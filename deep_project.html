Introduction¶

Verbal communication can be hindered in noisy environments or to address technical limitations in video recordings with low-quality audio, such as interpreting surveillance videos.

It can also find applications in:

  • Protecting against audio deepfakes (through lip-reading)
  • in human-computer interaction, allowing devices to be controlled without speaking.

Further developments could include integration into augmented/virtual reality, social robotics, and the creation of lifelike avatars capable of faithfully reproducing speech movements.

In this context, Lip Reading emerges as a vital tool. This project focuses on the analysis of videos cropped exclusively around the lips, excluding audio. This approach is fundamental in training a neural network model focused on lip movements.

The dataset used for this analysis includes 650 videos distributed across 43 different subject classes. Each analyzed video was standardized to a length of 150 frames using zero-padding techniques.

The Data Exploration begins by scanning the dataset directory. Since the dataset is organized into subfolders, we use os.walk to navigate and extract the file.

For every video, we collect the following attributes:

  • User: Extracted from the subfolder name (the subject's identity).
  • Label: Extracted from the filename (the word being spoken), normalized to uppercase.
  • Frames: The total temporal length of the video.
  • Resolution (res): The spatial dimensions (Width x Height).
  • FPS: The fluidity of the recording (Frames Per Second).

This metadata is compiled into a list and subsequently converted into a DataFrame.

    for file_name in files:

            # 1. Estraiamo il nome della cartella corrente, che contiene il nome di chi ha fatto il video

            user = os.path.basename(root)

            video_path = os.path.join(root, file_name)

            cap.release()
            
            # 3. Riempiamo la lista
            data_list.append({
                'user': user,
                'file': file_name,
                'label': label,
                'frames': frame_count,
                'res': f"{width}x{height}",
                'fps': fps
            })

After analyzing the Frames Distribution, we observed that while the absolute maximum is 150 frames, most samples fall within 100 frames.

This decision offers the next main advantages:

  • Coverage: This limit effectively covers the majority of the samples across almost all classes.
  • Impact of Truncation: Only 4.77% of the video sequences exceed this limit. For these samples, the truncation primarily affects the final frames, which in most recordings contain redundant or silent lip movements.
  • Computational Efficiency: Reducing the sequence length from 150 to 82 frames allows for a significantly faster training process and more efficient memory management, without compromising the core linguistic features of the lip dynamics.

Data Splitting Strategy¶

Since the videos are divided by user folders (Adriano, Alessandra, etc.), we don't randomly divide the videos between the training set and the validation set, but instead apply a stratification by user, placing some users' folders in the Training set and other users' folders in the Validation set.

This ensures that the model truly learns to read lips regardless of who speaks, for example Adriano says 'FLAG' 5 times and 'SOFT' only 2 times, so the model could classify in 'FLAG' when it sees a video of Adriano.

This approach will make the model much more robust. Then, the folders by user will be randomly divided into the Training and Validation sets (80% - 20%).
